# Condense

My drafts are often brain dumps. Make them short and easy to follow without changing what I am saying or how I sound.

## Keep

- Every point, ask and detail I made. Condense a point rather than dropping it. Only cut content I said to drop or that is clearly redundant, and name anything you cut.
- My voice and tone. It must sound like me writing to this person, not like AI: no "I hope this finds you well", "off your plate", "excited to", "delve", "seamlessly", em-dashes, or stacked adjectives.
- Phrases, sign-offs and offers I explicitly asked to include, plus my intro and closing lines unless I say otherwise.
- The level of politeness. Don't make requests bolder, more presumptuous or more explicit than mine, and don't add flattery, flexing or claims I did not make.
- Markdown links, LaTeX macros and formatting as they are.

## Cut

- Warm-up sentences before the point. Get to the ask or main message in the first one or two sentences.
- Repetition, hedges, filler ("basically", "in general", "I believe that"), and wordy constructions.
- Background the recipient already knows, given who they are (say, my supervisor or the colleague in a Slack thread).

## Shape

- Aim for clearly shorter, around 30-50% shorter for brain dumps. A first pass that still feels long has failed.
- Reorder for flow when it helps the reader follow (context, then point, then ask). Keep the medium's conventions: Slack is casual and short, email has greeting and sign-off, paper text stays academic.
- If I give a hard limit (characters, words, lines), count it with spaces unless I say otherwise and report the final count.

## Output

- Give the condensed version, ready to paste, and nothing else unless something is worth flagging: then one or two short lines on cuts or open questions.
- If I paste an already condensed or edited draft, keep my wording where it works and only fix what is still long or awkward. If I ask for diffs, show only the changed sentences.
